Infrastructure should be built for what is coming.

A business may be moving from legacy systems toward a hybrid or cloud-ready environment while AI introduces a new set of demands. The strongest answer looks ahead, then tests whether the infrastructure can carry what comes next.

01 / Clouds that scale

Bring capacity where it is needed.

Cloud and private environments should let the business add the right capacity as demand changes, with a clear path for each new requirement.

02 / Network built for AI traffic

Keep decisions moving in real time.

Bandwidth, latency, resiliency, and traffic visibility shape whether AI can support a live interaction or becomes another slow handoff. A network delivered, operated, and visible as one accountable service gives the business a cleaner way to keep pace.

03 / Edge & compute

Place processing where it performs best.

Data sensitivity, response time, and compute density determine when capacity belongs in the cloud, closer to the edge, or in a more dedicated and interconnected environment.

04 / Spend visibility

See the economics as usage grows.

Cloud, compute, token, license, and integration costs belong in one operating view so leadership can see what the environment requires and what it returns.

Most organizations are moving from what they have toward what AI requires.

Legacy

Built for the applications it has carried.

Familiar systems, fixed capacity, and an environment designed around the applications already in place.

Hybrid

New environments begin to connect.

Cloud and on-premise coexist, while connectivity and placement choices become part of the operating conversation.

Cloud-ready

Capacity and control can scale.

The business can add capacity, manage connectivity, and see the environment well enough to make deliberate choices.

AI-ready

Each workload has a deliberate home.

Network, compute, placement, and economics support the business at the speed and scale it requires.

AI can demand 10x the compute density.

AI inference and training rely on GPU-accelerated environments that most on-premise data centers were never designed to support. The choice is not simply where AI runs. It is whether to consume capacity, own the environment, or deliberately combine both.

Consume it or own it?

Start with the workload and the data behind it. Flexible work can consume capacity as a service. Sensitive, regulated, or residency-bound data may need an environment you control.

Consume

Use GPU capacity as a service.

Best for bursty, experimental, or flexible workloads where speed and elasticity matter most.

Own

Keep sensitive work in an environment you control.

Healthcare, legal, and financial services may need private capacity for protected data, performance, residency, or sustained demand.

Blend

Keep some data close. Scale some compute out.

Use a hybrid design when the work needs both controlled data and elastic compute.

Build the environment the work requires.
