Governance & security

Protect the work.
Keep what matters.

Protect your people, information, and systems. Give AI clear boundaries—and make sure someone owns what happens next.

What this means for your business ↓

Protect the business you run today

Make everyday work safer.
Give your team room to move.

A shared file, a browser tab, a customer portal. Start where work already happens, then extend the protection as AI connects to more of the business.

Do you know what sensitive data you hold?+

Locate and classify sensitive information across cloud services, local systems, shared files, email, and collaboration tools. Include customer and employee records, financial information, intellectual property, and the documents people work with every day.

Data security posture management (DSPM) helps identify sensitive data and assess its exposure. Review excessive access, forgotten copies, and changes over time so findings lead to fixes.

What can your AI tools reach that their users should not?

Do you know where sensitive information is going?+

Find the AI tools, browser extensions, and cloud applications people actually use—including unapproved tools. Identify what gets pasted, uploaded, downloaded, or shared, and give people an approved way to do the work.

Evaluate browser controls and data loss prevention that can warn, restrict, or block sensitive transfers where deployed. Check coverage across managed devices, personal devices, and contractors.

Can people get help before they share something they should not?

Does access stop where the job stops?+

Use strong sign-in, multi-factor authentication, and access matched to each role. Review employee, contractor, service-account, and agent permissions as responsibilities change.

Who can reach critical applications—and how quickly can you remove that access?

Are the connections into your business protected?+

Inventory APIs—the connections through which applications and agents exchange information. Know who owns them, what data they expose, and whether their permissions and behavior match the intended use.

Evaluate application and API protection, security testing, bot controls, and defenses against traffic that overwhelms a service. Connect findings to a team that can investigate and fix them.

Which exposed connection could interrupt service or reveal customer information?

If one system is compromised, how far can it spread?+

Separate critical systems and limit which workloads can communicate. Microsegmentation creates finer boundaries within the environment, helping restrict an intruder’s movement after an initial breach.

Could a compromised workstation reach payroll, customer records, or production systems it does not need?

In practice

Clear boundaries make useful work possible.

Bring business, IT, security, and privacy or legal owners into the same conversation. Agree on what people can do, where information may go, and who is accountable for the decisions and actions it influences.

Know what may enter.

Classify information and define approved tools and environments. Confirm the right to use the data for the intended purpose. Review privacy, data residency, customer contracts, and supplier terms for storage and retention with the relevant owners.

Set the authority.

Decide what a person or agent may retrieve, connect to, change, or trigger. Require human approval for consequential actions and name the person accountable for the outcome.

Ask how a reviewer can inspect the evidence, challenge a recommendation, and escalate an uncertain case. The provider should explain when its system withholds a recommendation and how decisions and overrides are recorded.

Test what can cross the boundary.

Evaluate input and output controls for malicious instructions, sensitive-data exposure, and misuse. Test them against your workflows. Filters support security; they do not establish that an answer is accurate or an action is authorized.

Retain with intention.

Preserve reusable knowledge where appropriate. Define what should be captured, who can access it, and when it should be reviewed or removed. Separate useful business knowledge from sensitive interaction logs; neither should be kept indiscriminately.

When something goes wrong

Who sees it?
Who takes the next step?

Security tools need people, authority, and a practiced response behind them. This matters whether your AI plans are early or already in production.

Know who is watching.

Connect identity, endpoint, application, and AI alerts to a clear monitoring responsibility. Compare internal coverage with managed detection and response, including nights, weekends, and escalation.

Rehearse the response.

Name who can isolate a system, revoke access, or pause an agent. Exercise the incident plan and confirm who coordinates technical recovery and stakeholder communications.

Prove you can recover.

Test protected backups and restoration against the downtime and data loss the business can tolerate. Keep evidence of access reviews, control tests, and incident follow-up for customer, audit, and insurance discussions.
